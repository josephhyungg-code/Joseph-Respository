## [BBC News] Anti-migrant protests spread from Dover to Portsmouth, minister condemns "thuggish" behaviour

Hampshire's police and crime commissioner, Donna Jones, told the BBC the unrest was a "wake-up call" for the government, while UK minister Emma Reynolds condemned the protesters' behaviour as "thuggish and intimidating."   - Officers held the cordon for nearly three hours before the crowd dispersed.
  - (한국어: 저지선)

  - The minister condemned the violence as "thuggish and intimidating."
  - Human rights groups condemned the new law within hours of its passing.
  - He refused to condemn the protest, saying people had a right to be angry.
- **disperse** — to (make people) scatter and leave an area
  - Police ordered the crowd to disperse or face arrest.
  - The protesters slowly dispersed once the buses had left.
  - Tear gas is sometimes used to disperse large crowds.
- **standoff** — a tense situation in which two opposing sides refuse to back down
  - The standoff between police and demonstrators lasted for hours.
  - Talks ended in a standoff, with neither company willing to compromise.
  - A tense standoff outside the port delayed the ferry's departure.
- **asylum seeker** — a person who has left their home country and is asking a government to protect them as a refugee

  - Portsmouth lacks the same infrastructure Dover uses to process new arrivals.

  - The commissioner called the protests "a wake-up call" for the government.
  - The factory fire was a wake-up call about outdated safety rules.

## [CNN] A billion flies are now in the screwworm fight. Will there be enough?

- Link: https://edition.cnn.com/2026/09/07/health/screwworm-billion-flies
- **Summary:** The US government has now released its billionth sterile fly in Texas, part of a strategy to stop the New World screwworm — a flesh-eating livestock pest — from spreading north from Mexico. The flies, bred at new facilities in Chiapas, Mexico, and in Panama, are sterilized and released to mate with wild screwworm flies, producing no offspring and gradually collapsing the wild population. Experts warn the pest reproduces explosively — a female can lay up to 3,000 eggs in her roughly 21-day life — and point to the 1970s, when cases jumped from 473 to nearly 100,000 in a single year, as a warning of how fast an outbreak can grow. Officials are divided: some worry current fly-production capacity isn't enough, while a senior USDA official says early field results are encouraging.

**Key Vocabulary (7 words)**
- **sterile** /STER-uhl/ — unable to reproduce; also, free of germs
  - Sterile flies are released so they cannot produce offspring with wild ones.
  - Surgeons must use sterile instruments to avoid infection.
  - The soil here has become sterile after years of drought.
  - (한국어: 불임의)
- **outpace** — to grow or move faster than something else
  - Cases have started to outpace the country's ability to respond.
  - Demand for the vaccine is outpacing supply.
  - Her savings are outpacing her spending for the first time in years.
- **infestation** — a situation in which a place or animal is overrun by pests
  - Ranchers reported a screwworm infestation in several herds near the border.
  - The old building had a serious termite infestation.
  - Doctors treated the wound after a maggot infestation set in.
- **livestock** — farm animals raised for meat, milk, or labor
  - The screwworm can cause serious wounds in cattle and other livestock.
  - Livestock farmers are watching the outbreak closely.
  - Drought has made it harder to feed livestock this year.
- **eradicate** — to completely destroy or get rid of something, especially a disease or pest
  - The program successfully eradicated screwworm from the US decades ago.
  - Scientists hope to eradicate the disease within a generation.
  - It took a global effort to eradicate smallpox.
- **capacity** — the maximum amount something can produce or hold
  - "We just can't produce enough flies with what we have right now," one expert said of production capacity.
  - The hospital is operating at full capacity.
  - Factory capacity was doubled to meet demand.
- **precedent** — an earlier event used as an example or guide for the present
  - Experts point to the 1970s outbreak as a historical precedent for how fast cases can rise.
  - The court decision set a legal precedent.
  - There's no precedent for a storm this size in the region.

**Useful Expressions (3)**
- **keep pace with** — to move or increase at the same speed as something else
  - Fly production must keep pace with the screwworm's rapid reproduction.
  - Wages haven't kept pace with rising prices.
- **field test** — a real-world trial of something outside the laboratory
  - Field tests of the release program are showing encouraging results.
  - The new drug is now undergoing field tests in three countries.
- **on track to** — currently moving toward a likely future result
  - The outbreak is on track to become one of the costliest ever recorded.
  - The project is on track to finish by December.

## Politics & Current Affairs Terms

*(Included because today's set covers UK migration protests and a Singapore-Luxembourg diplomatic visit.)*

- **asylum seeker** — someone requesting protection as a refugee in another country
  - The government is building more capacity to process asylum seekers.
  - Common in headlines and formal government statements alike.
- **processing centre** — a facility where new arrivals or claims are formally registered and reviewed
  - The migrants were taken to a processing centre in Kent.
  - Common in news reports on migration policy.
- **condemn** — to formally and publicly criticize something as wrong
  - The Home Office condemned the violence at the protest.
  - Very common in official government and diplomatic statements.
- **dispersal order** — a legal order allowing police to require a crowd to leave an area
  - Police issued a dispersal order to clear the port area.
  - Formal/legal term, common in UK police statements.
- **bilateral ties** — the relationship between two countries
  - Leaders pledged to strengthen bilateral ties between the two nations.
  - Extremely common in diplomatic and headline English.

  - Singapore's ASEAN chairmanship begins next year.
  - Common in regional diplomacy coverage, especially in Asia.
