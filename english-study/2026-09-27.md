### [CNN] UK police arrest five men on suspicion of terror offenses near air base used by US forces
- Link: https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/27/uk/fairford-arrests-uk-raf-us-intl
- **Summary:** In the early hours of Sunday, armed police stopped three suspicious vehicles heading toward RAF Fairford, a UK air base used by US B-1B bombers during recent strikes on Iran. Five men were arrested on suspicion of explosives offenses and, later, of preparing a terrorist act. About 85 nearby homes were evacuated as an Army bomb-disposal unit examined the vehicles behind a 400-metre cordon. Investigators are now looking into whether the plot is connected to Iran, whose Revolutionary Guard had called the base a "legitimate target."

**Key Vocabulary (7 words)**
- **suspicion** /səˈspɪʃ.ən/ — a feeling that something is wrong or that someone may have broken the law
  - Police arrested him on suspicion of theft.
  - There's growing suspicion that the fire was started deliberately.
  - She looked at the stranger with suspicion.
- **explosives** — materials that can cause a sudden, violent blast
  - The van was found to be carrying explosives.
  - Bomb-disposal teams are trained to handle explosives safely.
  - Possessing explosives without a license is a serious crime.
- **cordon** — a line of police or barriers set up to keep people out of an area
  - Officers set up a cordon around the crime scene.
  - The area behind the cordon is closed to traffic.
  - Residents were kept outside the security cordon for hours.
- **evacuate** — to move people away from a dangerous place to somewhere safer
  - Authorities evacuated the coastal town before the storm hit.
  - The building was evacuated after the fire alarm went off.
  - Around 85 households were evacuated as a precaution.
- **custody** (한국어: 구금) — the state of being held by police, especially before a trial
  - The suspects remain in police custody.
  - He was taken into custody shortly after the incident.
  - She was released from custody after questioning.
- **legitimate** — allowed by law, or reasonable and acceptable
  - The group claimed the base was a legitimate military target.
  - He has a legitimate reason for being late.
  - It's a legitimate question to ask.
- **inquiry** — an official process of asking questions to find out facts
  - Police have launched an inquiry into the incident.
  - The inquiry will take several months to complete.
  - One line of inquiry is whether the men acted alone.

**Useful Expressions (3)**
- **on suspicion of (something)** — believed to have possibly done something illegal
  - He was arrested on suspicion of drunk driving.
  - The men were held on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act.
- **declare a major incident** — to officially announce that an emergency is serious and needs a large response
  - The council declared a major incident after the flooding began.
  - Police declared a major incident near the air base early Sunday.
- **carry out (a plan/plot)** — to actually do or complete something that was planned
  - Investigators want to know who paid them to carry out the plot.
  - The team carried out the rescue operation smoothly.

## Politics & Current Affairs Terms

Today's articles touched on diplomacy and security, so here are terms worth
knowing:

- **state visit** — an official, ceremonial visit by one head of state to another country
  - The president's state visit included a formal dinner at the palace.
  - *Common in headlines about diplomatic trips.*
- **trade truce** — a temporary pause in a trade dispute between countries
  - The trade truce gave both sides more time to negotiate.
  - *Common in business/politics headlines, especially US-China coverage.*
- **counter-terrorism** — organized efforts by government or police to prevent terrorist attacks
  - Counter-terrorism police are leading the investigation.
  - *Used constantly in UK and international security headlines.*
- **regime** — the government of a country, often used for one seen as authoritarian or not freely elected
  - The report accused the regime of funding the plot.
  - *Common in formal news writing and diplomatic speech, often with a critical tone.*
- **midterm elections** — US elections held roughly halfway through a president's four-year term
  - The visit came six weeks before the midterm elections.
  - *A staple term in American political headlines.*
- **delegation** — see vocabulary above; also a general political/diplomatic term
  - *Common in formal diplomatic and negotiation coverage.*
