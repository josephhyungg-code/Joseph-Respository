### [BBC News] Andy Burnham Set to Become Britain's Next Prime Minister

- Link: https://www.bbc.com/news (Politics section — "Andy Burnham poised to become UK prime minister")
- **Summary**: Andy Burnham, the former Mayor of Greater Manchester, has received nominations from 322 of the Labour Party's 403 MPs to succeed Sir Keir Starmer as party leader — just one short of the number that would make him mathematically unbeatable. Starmer resigned last month after poor local election results triggered calls for new leadership. Other potential challengers, including former defence minister Al Carns, have ruled themselves out, leaving Burnham as the only declared candidate. If no rival emerges by next Wednesday's deadline, Burnham is expected to be confirmed as Labour leader and formally become prime minister on 20 July.

**Key Vocabulary (7 words)**

- **nomination** /nom-uh-NAY-shuhn/ — formal support or backing put forward for someone to hold a position
  - He received nominations from over 300 members of parliament.
  - Her nomination for the award surprised no one; she'd earned it.
  - The nomination period closes at the end of the week.
- **succeed** — to take over a position or role after someone else (not "to be successful," in this context)
  - Burnham is expected to succeed Starmer as party leader.
  - The vice president will succeed the president if he resigns.
  - Who will succeed her as CEO when she retires?
- **threshold** — the point or level at which something changes or takes effect
  - He is one vote short of the threshold needed to win outright.
  - The company's profits finally crossed the threshold for a tax increase.
  - There's a pain threshold beyond which most people give up.
- **rival** — a person or group competing against another for the same goal
  - No serious rival has emerged to challenge him for the leadership.
  - The two companies have been fierce rivals for a decade.
  - She trained hard to beat her closest rival at the championship.
- **resign** — to formally give up a job or position
  - Starmer resigned as party leader after a difficult year in the polls.
  - He resigned from his post following the scandal.
  - She decided to resign rather than accept the new terms.
  - (한국어: 사임하다)
- **coronation** — literally, the ceremony of crowning a monarch; used loosely for an unopposed, near-certain rise to a top position
  - Commentators are already calling this Burnham's coronation, since no one is running against him.
  - The unopposed vote felt more like a coronation than a real contest.
  - Political writers love using "coronation" for uncontested leadership races.
- **outright** — completely, or without any doubt or competition
  - He needs just one more nomination to win outright.
  - She won the race outright, finishing minutes ahead of everyone else.
  - The board rejected the proposal outright.

**Useful Expressions (3)**

- **fall short (of something)** — to fail to reach a required amount or standard
  - He fell one nomination short of the threshold.
  - The company's earnings fell short of analysts' expectations.
- **rule oneself out** — to publicly withdraw from being considered for something
  - Al Carns ruled himself out of the leadership race on Wednesday.
  - She ruled herself out of the running for team captain.
- **take office** — to formally begin a position of authority, especially a political one
  - Burnham is due to take office as prime minister on 20 July.
  - The new mayor will take office next month.

**Comprehension Check**
1. Why is Andy Burnham considered very likely to become the next Labour leader and prime minister?
2. What event led to Keir Starmer's resignation?

### [CNN] Tensions Are Heating Up Over a Remote Arctic Territory — And It's Not Greenland

- Link: https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/10/climate/svalbard-greenland-geopolitics-russia-china-melting-ice
- **Summary**: On the Arctic archipelago of Svalbard, Norway has quietly removed a pair of stone lion statues and a sign reading "Yellow River Station" from a Chinese-run research outpost, in a move seen as reasserting Norwegian sovereignty. A century-old treaty gives Norway full control of Svalbard but allows nearly 50 signatory nations, including China and Russia, to live and work there visa-free. Tensions rose further after Chinese tourists, some with military ties, staged a patriotic display with flags and banners at the same settlement. As Arctic ice melts and shipping routes and resources become more accessible, Svalbard has become an unexpected new flashpoint in great-power competition.

**Key Vocabulary (7 words)**

- **sovereignty** /SOV-rin-tee/ — a nation's full authority and control over its own territory
  - Norway is trying to reinforce its sovereignty over Svalbard.
  - The treaty recognizes the country's sovereignty while granting other nations limited access.
  - Disputes over sovereignty are common in remote or contested regions.
- **archipelago** /ar-kuh-PEL-uh-go/ — a group or chain of islands
  - Svalbard is an archipelago located between Norway and the North Pole.
  - The Philippines is an archipelago made up of thousands of islands.
  - Tourists sailed through the archipelago, stopping at several small islands.
- **outpost** — a small settlement or base located far from the main territory it belongs to
  - China's research outpost in Svalbard has operated since 2004.
  - The remote military outpost had only a handful of soldiers stationed there.
  - Scientists live year-round at the isolated Antarctic outpost.
- **treaty** — a formal, legally binding agreement between countries
  - A century-old treaty governs who can live and work on Svalbard.
  - The two nations signed a treaty to end decades of conflict.
  - The climate treaty commits countries to cutting emissions.
- **flashpoint** — a place or situation likely to trigger sudden conflict
  - Svalbard is becoming a new flashpoint in Arctic competition.
  - The border region has long been a flashpoint between the two countries.
  - Analysts worry the disputed island could become the next flashpoint.
- **reassert** — to state or demonstrate something again, firmly, especially after it has been questioned
  - Norway removed the statues to reassert control over the settlement.
  - The champion reasserted her dominance by winning three matches in a row.
  - The government reasserted its authority after the protests.
- **patriotic** — expressing strong love and loyal support for one's own country
  - The tourists carried patriotic banners and national flags.
  - He gave a patriotic speech honoring the country's history.
  - The song is considered deeply patriotic by many citizens.

**Useful Expressions (3)**

- **stir up (tension/controversy)** — to cause a strong emotional or political reaction
  - The tourists' display stirred up tension between Norway and China.
  - His comments stirred up controversy online within minutes.
- **tangled up in something** — deeply and messily involved in a complicated situation
  - The quiet research station found itself tangled up in a geopolitical dispute.
  - Their finances got tangled up in a long legal battle.
- **keep an eye on** — to watch something carefully because it may become important
  - Diplomats are keeping a close eye on Arctic territorial disputes.
  - Investors are keeping an eye on interest rate announcements this week.

**Comprehension Check**
1. What did Norway remove from the Chinese research settlement, and why is this significant?
2. What does the century-old Svalbard treaty allow signatory countries to do?

### [CNA] Taxi and Private-Hire Drivers to Get New Support Package for Autonomous Vehicle Transition

- Link: https://www.channelnewsasia.com/singapore (Singapore section — "Career conversion programmes, up to S$1,600 in training incentives to help taxi, private-hire drivers amid AV transition")
- **Summary**: Singapore's Ministry of Transport, together with the Ministry of Manpower, NTUC, Grab and ComfortDelGro, has unveiled a support package to help taxi and private-hire drivers adapt as autonomous vehicles (AVs) enter the transport sector. From the third quarter of 2026, drivers can join a new career conversion programme to become AV specialists — such as safety operators or remote operators — or a separate pathway to become public bus captains, with up to 90% salary support during retraining. A training incentive of $20 an hour, capped at $1,600 per driver, will help offset lost income and vehicle rental costs. Officials say the AV rollout will be gradual, so drivers are not expected to be displaced in the short term.

**Key Vocabulary (7 words)**

- **transition** — the process of changing from one state or system to another
  - The package supports drivers through the transition to autonomous vehicles.
  - Singapore is managing a gradual transition to clean energy.
  - The transition from school to work can be difficult for young people.
- **incentive** — something offered to encourage a person to do something
  - Drivers can receive a training incentive of up to $1,600.
  - The company offered a cash incentive to boost sales.
  - Tax incentives encourage businesses to invest locally.
- **displace** — to force someone out of a job, role, or place, especially because of change
  - Officials say drivers won't be displaced by AVs in the short term.
  - Automation has displaced many factory workers over the years.
  - The flood displaced hundreds of families from their homes.
- **defray** — to provide money to pay part or all of a cost
  - The incentive helps defray income lost during training.
  - The scholarship defrays the cost of tuition for many students.
  - The company offered a stipend to defray travel expenses.
- **rollout** — the introduction of a new product, policy, or system, usually in stages
  - The AV rollout in Singapore will happen gradually.
  - The vaccine rollout reached rural areas within a few months.
  - The software rollout is planned for early next year.
- **upskill** — to learn new or advanced skills, usually for a job
  - Drivers can upskill to work as AV safety operators.
  - Many companies now offer programmes to upskill their employees.
  - She decided to upskill in data analysis to stay competitive.
- **pathway** — a planned route or set of steps toward a goal, especially in a career
  - The programme offers a pathway to becoming a bus captain.
  - There's a clear pathway from intern to full-time engineer at this firm.
  - The scholarship provides a pathway to university for low-income students.

**Useful Expressions (3)**

- **stay ahead of (something)** — to keep up with or anticipate changes before they cause problems
  - The programmes are designed to help drivers stay ahead of changes in technology.
  - Companies must innovate constantly to stay ahead of the competition.
- **in the short term** — over a near, immediate period of time (contrasted with "long term")
  - Drivers aren't expected to lose their jobs in the short term.
  - In the short term, prices may rise before they stabilize.
- **roll out (a programme/policy)** — to introduce something gradually and officially
  - The government will roll out the new career programmes in Q3 2026.
  - The city plans to roll out free WiFi across all subway stations.

**Comprehension Check**
1. What two career pathways are being offered to taxi and private-hire drivers under the new package?
2. Why do officials say drivers are not at risk of losing their jobs in the short term?

## Politics & Current Affairs Terms

- **leadership race** — a formal contest to determine who will lead a political party
  - The Labour leadership race is expected to end without a real contest, since Burnham faces no rival.
  - Note: very common in headlines ("leadership race," "leadership contest").
- **by-election** — a special election held to fill a single vacant seat between general elections
  - Burnham became an MP by winning a by-election in Makerfield in June.
  - Common in UK political reporting; less used in American English (which favors "special election").
- **mandate** — the authority given to a leader or government by voters or supporters to act on their behalf
  - Winning nearly every nomination gives Burnham a strong mandate to lead the party.
  - Frequently used in formal political speech and analysis.
- **geopolitics** — the study or practice of international relations shaped by geography, power, and territory
  - Melting Arctic ice has turned Svalbard into a new site of geopolitics between major powers.
  - Common in headlines about international tension, especially involving resources or borders.
- **bilateral** — involving two countries or parties directly, rather than a larger group
  - Norway and China have discussed the Svalbard issue through bilateral diplomatic channels.
  - Frequently used in formal diplomatic and news language, often contrasted with "multilateral."
- **policy rollout** — the staged introduction of a new government policy or programme
  - Singapore's AV driver-support policy rollout begins in the third quarter of 2026.
  - Common in government and business news, especially for gradual, multi-phase initiatives.
