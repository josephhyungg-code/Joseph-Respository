## PSF cap vs market gap
The $2.2M ceiling sets a size-dependent PSF cap: ~**$2,045 psf at 1,076 sqft**, ~$1,833 at 1,200 sqft, ~$1,692 at 1,300 sqft, ~$1,294 at 1,700 sqft.
- **D15 freehold** trades **$1,900–2,700 psf → above the cap** for any ≥1,000 sqft unit. The gap has widened since June (The Line's 3BR went from a $2,054 psf outlier to $2,560+). Only boutique/older (Carpmael) or penthouse-area artifacts (Flamingo) still clear.
- **D19 freehold/999** trades **$1,500–2,050 psf → inside the cap** for 1,000–1,300 sqft. Gap ≈ zero. This is why every clean pass but one is D19.
- **D14 Paya Lebar freehold** ~$1,830–1,960 psf → inside the cap for compact 3BRs.

## Filter trade-offs — new implications (세대수 vs PSF vs 지역)
1. **Tenure is the binding constraint on the large-development angle.** Every 200+ unit project with a size+price-fitting 3BR is 99-yr leasehold (Kovan Regency/Residences/Melody, Silversea, Sanctuary Green, Costa Rhu, Water Place, Florence/Riverfront). The only large *freehold* ones price ≥1,000 sqft at $2.7M+. **You can have large-development liquidity + freehold + ≥1,000 sqft + ≤$2.2M — but only 3 of the 4.**
2. **Every clean pass is a boutique project** (16–140 units). The size+FH+budget triangle structurally forces you into low-unit-count developments with thin transaction data and low liquidity. That is the real cost of holding the tenure line.
3. **Region is the release valve.** Relaxing from D15 to **D19 / D14 Paya Lebar** is what makes family-size fit the budget at all — D15 freehold has re-rated above the cap.
4. **The "not a new launch" rule is now actively excluding otherwise-perfect stock:** Jansen House ($2.038M / 1,012 sqft / 999-yr / D19) passes every other axis and fails only on being a just-TOP'd launch. As 2024–26 boutique launches complete, this rule will keep rejecting the newest 999-yr resale — worth a decision on whether to relax it.
